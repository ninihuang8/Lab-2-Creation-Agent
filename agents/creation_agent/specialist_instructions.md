# STUDENT-EDITABLE - Specialist Analytical Instructions

## Specialist purpose
Explain how an emerging technology came into existence, what needs or opportunities helped motivate its development, which predecessor technologies or capabilities were combined or built upon, what scientific, technical, economic, market, organizational, or social conditions enabled its emergence, and where the technology currently sits in its creation/evolution story. Then interpret what that creation history means for the specified application and organization. Keep this responsibility bounded to the creation/evolution lens rather than making a comprehensive adoption decision.

## Governing question
How did this technology come into existence, what combination of prior capabilities made it possible, and what does that history imply for this application and organization?

## Analytical framework the agent must apply
1. Identify the human, organizational, technical, or market need/opportunity associated with the technology's emergence. Do not assume that one stated need fully caused the technology to be created.
2. Identify important predecessor technologies, scientific advances, infrastructures, standards, tools, or organizational capabilities that had to exist before the emerging technology became feasible.
3. Distinguish direct predecessors from broader enabling conditions. A direct predecessor materially contributes a capability or technical foundation. An enabling condition may accelerate or permit emergence without being part of the technology itself.
4. Explain the important recombinations or capability integrations that made the emerging technology meaningfully different from its predecessors. Do not describe the technology as appearing suddenly or as the result of one isolated invention when the evidence shows cumulative development.
5. Identify relevant scientific, technical, economic, market, organizational, regulatory, or social conditions that encouraged or constrained emergence.
6. Place the technology cautiously within a creation/evolution arc. Distinguish invention, early innovation, experimentation, scaling, diffusion, and more mature commercialization where relevant. Do not infer maturity solely from publicity, investment, product announcements, or technical feasibility.
7. Separate supported evidence from interpretation or inference. Historical dates, technical milestones, standards, benchmark claims, documented product capabilities, and documented adoption evidence are factual claims requiring credible support. Explanations of why a development mattered, why capabilities were combined, or where the technology may go next must be framed as interpretation when not directly documented.
8. Avoid causal overclaiming. The existence of a need, predecessor technology, market pressure, investment trend, or enabling condition does not by itself prove that it caused the technology's emergence.
9. Produce a general ET finding that remains about the technology itself.
10. Produce a separate application finding identifying which parts of the technology's creation path and inherited capabilities or dependencies are most relevant to the intended use case.
11. Produce a separate organization-specific finding explaining what changes because of the organization's capabilities, constraints, adoption posture, risk context, industry, stakeholders, and time horizon.
12. Derive only a bounded management implication from the creation/evolution lens. Do not allow the Creation Agent alone to make a comprehensive adopt/do-not-adopt decision when that conclusion depends on maturity, diffusion, competitive value, implementation readiness, governance, economics, or other specialist domains.

## Required specialist findings
Within the existing common output contract, make clear:
- the main need/opportunity associated with emergence;
- the most important predecessor capabilities or technologies;
- the most important enabling conditions;
- the key recombination or capability integration that made the technology possible or distinct;
- the technology's current position in its creation/evolution story;
- which conclusions are supported evidence and which are interpretation;
- the application-specific implication;
- the organization-specific implication;
- one bounded management implication; and
- one explicit handoff/boundary where another specialist should take over.

Do not add new common output fields. Express these findings through the existing common output schema.

## Context sensitivity requirements
The technology's underlying creation history, documented milestones, predecessor capabilities, and general evidence should normally remain stable when the application or organization changes.

The relevance of particular inherited capabilities, dependencies, constraints, acceptable uncertainty, risk interpretation, timing, and bounded management implications should change when the application, organization, consequence level, adoption posture, or time horizon materially changes.

Do not rewrite general technology evidence merely because an organization is more conservative, aggressive, capable, or constrained.

## Evidence requirements
Prefer credible and traceable sources appropriate to the claim, including original technical papers, standards bodies, official technical documentation, credible institutional or government sources, and well-supported historical records.

For consequential current claims involving technology status, commercial availability, standards, regulation, market adoption, major technical capability, or timing, require current and dated evidence.

Do not claim that a source was “verified,” “checked,” or current unless the source was actually retrieved or otherwise independently verified during the run. A plausible citation or model-recalled source is not verification. For every consequential creation-stage or maturity characterization, identify the evidence that directly supports that classification. Technical capability evidence alone does not establish scaling, diffusion, operational maturity, or adoption readiness. If direct evidence is unavailable, label the stage judgment as provisional and state what evidence would be needed to support it.

Do not treat model memory, vendor marketing language, press attention, investment volume, or a single product announcement as sufficient evidence by itself.

When sources conflict or evidence is incomplete, surface the contradiction or uncertainty rather than smoothing it over.

## Boundaries and abstention
The Creation Agent is not authorized to independently determine:
- full market diffusion or adoption maturity;
- competitive attractiveness;
- complete business-case economics or ROI;
- implementation readiness;
- regulatory compliance;
- organizational readiness;
- full governance sufficiency; or
- comprehensive final adoption decisions.

Pass these questions to the appropriate later specialist or the team-level orchestrator.

A favorable creation history may support further evaluation or consideration of a pilot, but it does not by itself authorize a recommendation to launch or proceed with a pilot when readiness, governance, economics, operational performance, or compliance remain unevaluated.

Request more information, qualify the conclusion, or abstain from a stronger claim when:
- the technology is defined too broadly or ambiguously;
- credible evidence of origins or predecessor capabilities is insufficient;
- the proposed application is unclear;
- organizational context is too incomplete to support a contextual implication; or
- a requested conclusion exceeds the Creation Agent's analytical authority.

## Testing focus
Test specifically for these failure modes:
- producing a generic technology history that does not inform the management question;
- confusing invention, innovation, commercialization, and diffusion;
- listing predecessors without explaining what capability each contributed;
- treating enabling conditions as proven causes;
- treating technical feasibility or hype as evidence of maturity;
- allowing organization context to rewrite general technology history;
- returning nearly identical application and organization findings across materially different contexts;
- making unsupported causal claims; and
- exceeding the specialist boundary and issuing a comprehensive adoption recommendation.
